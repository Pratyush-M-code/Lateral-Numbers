What is this project about?

This project is to help build an intuitive understanding of Complex numbers and operations.
This project is based on this lecture by 3 Blue 1 Brown and is designed to provide an interactive way to explore complex number concepts that he teaches in his lecture series.
It is made for people who want to have a more visula understanding of complex numbers!

What are Complex Numbers?

Complex numbers are numbers that have a real and imaginary component. They can be represented in the form of (a + bi) where a is the real component and b is the imaginary component.

Complex numbers can be represented on a cartesian plane with the x-axis representing the real component and the y-axis representing the imaginary component.

What is complex addition?

Complex addition is simply the addition of the real and imaginary coefficients of the 2 complex numbers independently.
For example:- (a + bi) + (c + di)
= (a + c) + (b + d)i

In the graph, vector 1 is first made from its components, then vector 2 is made from its components. Vector 2 is then joined to the tip of vector 1, and the real and imaginary components are added independently to form the resultant!

What is complex subtraction?

Complex subtraction is simply the subtraction of the real and imaginary coefficients of the 2 complex numbers independently.
For example:- (a + bi) - (c + di)
= (a - c) + (b - d)i

Visualize!

In the graph, vector 1 is first made from its components, then vector 2 is made from its components. Vector 2 is then joined to the tip of vector 1 and flipped to point the opposite way, which is the same as multiplying it by -1. Finally, the flipped vector is added to vector 1 to form the resultant, because subtracting a number is the same as adding its negative!

What is Euler's form?

Any complex number can be written using its length and its angle.
For example:- a + bi = r·eiθ
where r = √(a² + b²) and θ = atan2(b, a)

Visualize!

In the graph, the vector a + bi is first made from its components. A circle is then drawn through its tip, and its radius is r. Finally, the angle θ is measured from the real axis up to the vector. Together, r and θ give the number in Euler's form!

What is complex multiplication?

Complex multiplication is the process of multiplying two complex numbers.
For example:- (a + bi) * (c + di)
= (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i

Visualize!

In the graph, vector 1 is first drawn, then vector 2 is drawn. To multiply the two vectors, the intuitive way to think about it is to transform the grid itself. The grid is rotated and stretched so that the number 1 on the real axis lands on vector 1, which means the grid is turned by the angle of vector 1 and scaled by its length. Vector 2 is carried along with the grid, and where it ends up is the product. In other words, multiplying adds the angles and multiplies the lengths!

What is complex division?

Complex division is the process of dividing two complex numbers.
For example:- (a + bi) / (c + di)
= ((ac + bd) / (c² + d²)) + ((bc - ad) / (c² + d²))i

Visualize!

In the graph, vector 1 is first drawn, then vector 2 is drawn. Division is multiplication in reverse, so the intuitive way to think about it is to transform the grid itself. The grid is rotated and shrunk or stretched until vector 2 lands exactly on the number 1 on the real axis, which means the grid is turned backwards by the angle of vector 2 and scaled by one over its length. Vector 1 is carried along with the grid, and where it ends up is the quotient. In other words, dividing subtracts the angles and divides the lengths!

What is complex exponentiation?

Complex exponentiation by an integer is repeated multiplication of a complex number by itself.
For example:- (a + bi)^n
= r^n * (cos(nθ) + i·sin(nθ)), where r is the magnitude and θ is the argument of (a + bi)

Visualize!

In the graph, vector 1 is drawn first. Raising it to the power n is just multiplying the grid by vector 1, n - 1 more times in a row — so the grid rotates by θ and scales by r for every extra power. The vector sweeps along with the grid, ending n times further rotated and r^n times longer than where it started. In other words, exponentiation multiplies the angle by n and raises the length to the nth power!

How should I use this?

Currently this project is extremely basic.

4 Operations are accepted =>
- Addition(+): To add 2 complex numbers together
- Subtraction(-): To subtract 2 complex numbers.
- Multiplication(*): To scale the cartestian plane to multiply 2 complex numbers together.
- Division(/): To scale the cartestian plane with respect to a vector to divide 2 complex numbers together.
- Exponentiation(^): To scale the cartestian plane with respect to a real vector to exponentiate a complex number. To use this you must use (a + 0i) as the second operant.

Keep in Mind

- All complex numbers are to be declared in the following format => (a + bi)
- There may be some bugs, if you find any, please contact me here.

Examples to try out

-> (3 + 2i) + (4 + 1i)
-> (3 + 2i) - (4 - 1i)
-> (2 + 4i) / (3 - 7i)
-> (5 - 3i) + (2 + 6i)
-> (1 + 2i) * (1 - 2i)
